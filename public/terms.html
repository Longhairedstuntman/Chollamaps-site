3What Cholla is

Cholla helps people plan and navigate trips on public land in the Mountain West: trail, road and land-ownership maps, motorized-route (MVUM) data, snow, avalanche and slope-angle layers, weather, offline maps, activity recording, routes, and group features (“crews”) including location sharing and check-ins. Some features are free; some require a Cholla Pro subscription.

Cholla is a planning and navigation aid built on public agency data and contributions from other users. It is not an official source of law, land status, trail conditions, weather, or avalanche danger, and it is not a safety or emergency service. See Section 4.

The data can be wrong, incomplete or out of date

  • Avalanche layer. The color shown for an avalanche zone is the day’s highest forecast danger for that whole zone, pulled from avalanche.org and CAIC and cached for up to ten minutes. It is a pointer, not the forecast. Always read the full, current forecast from the responsible avalanche center before entering avalanche terrain, and get proper training and equipment.
  • Slope angle. Slope shading is computed from 10-meter USGS elevation data. It smooths small terrain features and is a planning guide, not a measurement. Verify slope angle in the field.
  • Roads, gates, closures and seasons. Road status, gate status, closures and seasonal restrictions come from inconsistent public agency feeds and from user field reports. Field reports are unverified, submitted by other users, and age out over time. Data marked “unverified” or undated may be wrong. The Motor Vehicle Use Map (MVUM) is the Forest Service’s legal map — Cholla is not. You are responsible for knowing and obeying all motor-vehicle, land-use, seasonal and closure rules.
  • Land ownership & access. Land-ownership and boundary data is approximate. You are responsible for avoiding trespass and for confirming legal access before you travel.
  • Snow, weather & sensor readings. SNOTEL and similar readings are from sparse, individual sites and are not conditions at your location. Weather is a forecast.
  • Offline maps can fail. Downloads can be incomplete, expire, or be lost to a device or battery failure. Always carry a paper map and compass and know how to use them.

Crews, check-ins and messaging are not an emergency service

Location sharing, check-ins, overdue alerts, nearby/short-range messaging and any future radio or satellite messaging depend on signal and devices and may be delayed or may never arrive. They do not contact emergency services and must never be relied on as a safety or rescue system. Carry a dedicated satellite communicator or personal locator beacon, leave a trip plan with someone you trust, and know how to summon help without the Service.

By using the safety-related layers and features, you acknowledge these limitations and accept full responsibility for verifying conditions through official sources and for your own decisions.
